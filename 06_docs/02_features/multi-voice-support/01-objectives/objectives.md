## 0. What changes for the listener (plain language)

Today Watchpost Radio has one voice for everything. After 0.14.0:

- **Alerts sound different before a word is spoken.** Each class of alert has its own sound — five presets
  across six classes: warnings and disasters share the loudest; watches, advisories, special statements and
  tropical/winter storms each have their own; in Setup you can mute any class's tone, and `[M]` mutes them all
  at once (the words always read). This is on for everyone, even on a fresh install. *This is the fresh-install
  **cue** — it says **which class** is coming. The **answer** to the locked problem (alert vs report, heard in
  two seconds) is the cast below, and it is opt-in by MVS-D-8 (**MVS-D-31**, E-3).*
- **You can give the station a cast.** In Setup, the "WATCHPOST RADIO - CORRESPONDENTS" group lets you pick a voice for
  everything, for the alert reads, for the routine reports, or for any one report (weather, maritime, fire,
  seismic) and for the station's own lead and sign-off. Anything you don't set inherits from the level above.
  Every choice can be previewed there. The old `[V]` chooser goes away; its key opens that group.
- **A new maritime report** joins the broadcast for coastal places: the coastal-waters forecast, the nearest
  buoy's observations, tides and currents — the same shape as the fire and seismic reports, and just as
  overridable.
- **When correspondents change mid-broadcast, they hand over** ("This is Rishi, taking over for Samantha"),
  never a jarring splice; the diagnostics page (`[S]`) shows who reads what and why.
- **Nothing goes silent.** If a chosen voice isn't installed on this machine — or is a name from the other
  operating system — the next voice up the chain speaks, and the diagnostics page says so.
